# US-87.5 — A subscription names its rows

**Status:** Testing
**Phase:** 87 — The app gets fast
**Depends on:** nothing (publication migration + the 19 subscribing components)

## Story

As the operator paying for this database, **89.5% of all its execution time
is Realtime decoding write-ahead log records** — 28,016 s of 31,286 s
measured over six weeks on prod, 2,449,775 calls at 11.4 ms each. It is
123× more expensive than the next statement and 8.5× more than every other
query in the database combined. It is doing this for **12 live
subscriptions**.

Two causes, both ours.

The publication carries **27 tables**, including high-churn audit tables no
client subscribes to at scale — `agent_server_jobs` (9,125 rows),
`run_trace` (6,968), `runner_command_audit` (1,645),
`agent_session_events`, `deployment_run_events`. Every insert is decoded and
RLS-evaluated per subscriber whether anyone is listening or not.

And subscriptions are unfiltered. `shell-live-count.tsx` does it right
(`filter: org_id=eq.${orgId}`); `use-project-issues.ts` subscribes to
`table: "issues"` with no filter at all, so every issue change in every
workspace is decoded, RLS-checked, shipped to the browser, and discarded in
JS.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC1 — the publication carries only what is subscribed.** Each of the 27
  tables in `supabase_realtime` is checked against the 19 subscribing
  components; any table no client subscribes to is dropped from the
  publication by migration. The story lists each table with the component
  that justifies it, or the reason it was dropped.
- **AC2 — every subscription is filtered.** No `postgres_changes`
  subscription in `apps/web` subscribes to a bare table. Each carries at
  minimum `org_id=eq.<org>`, and narrower where the surface is narrower
  (issue id, run id, project id). `use-project-issues.ts` is the named
  offender.
- **AC3 — high-churn telemetry does not ride the WAL.** Where a surface
  needs live agent output rather than row changes (run trace, command
  audit, session events), it uses a Realtime *broadcast* channel or the
  existing control socket, not table replication. If a table must stay
  replicated, the story says why.
- **AC4 — nothing goes dark.** Every live surface still updates: the hub,
  the shell badge, notifications, the factory queue, run trace, the console,
  comments, documents, reports, team, deployments. Each is verified live,
  not assumed — a subscription silently filtered into nothing is the
  regression this story most risks.
- **AC5 — applied to both projects.** The publication migration lands in
  `infra/supabase/migrations/`, applied to **prod and dev**.
- **AC6 — proof.** `pg_stat_statements` is reset and the WAL decoder's share
  of total execution time is re-measured after a representative day. The
  before (89.5%) and after figures are recorded in the story.

## Out of scope

- Moving off Supabase Realtime.
- The `supabase_realtime_messages_publication` partitions (broadcast's own
  storage, managed by Supabase).
- Reducing the *volume* of writes into those tables — us-87.7 covers the
  worker heartbeat, the largest single writer.

## Verification record

Built 2026-08-12 on `phase-87-performance`. Migration 250, applied to **both**
prod and dev.

**Verified:** the publication reads **20 tables on both databases**, down from
27. Each of the seven dropped was checked against every `postgres_changes`
subscription in `apps/web` and has no subscriber; the migration lists all
twenty that remain against the component that justifies each. That check earned
its keep -- `clarifications` and `releases` look unsubscribed until you read
`shell-live-count.tsx`'s `TABLES` array, and dropping them would have silently
frozen the sidebar badge.

A block-accurate audit (parsing each `postgres_changes` config, not grepping
lines) reports **30 of 32 subscriptions now name their rows**. The two that do
not target `app_issues`, which is not in the publication at all, so they are
inert. `npm run build` type-checks clean.

**Not verified (UAT) -- and this is the story where that matters most:**

- **AC4 is NOT met.** Not one of the ~19 live surfaces was observed updating
  after this change. A subscription filtered into nothing goes dark with no
  error, no failed request and no console message -- "it looks fine" and "it is
  broken" are visually identical here. Every surface named in migration 250
  needs a live probe: change a row, watch it arrive.
- **AC6's re-measurement has not happened.** `pg_stat_statements` was not
  reset, so the 89.5% figure has no "after" beside it yet.

**Found, deliberately not fixed:** five tables are subscribed but were never in
the publication -- `issue_events`, `approvals`, `app_issues`, `agent_presets` --
so Live activity, the Activity feed, the system-issues console, the Reports hub
and the preset list do not update live today and have not for some time.
Publishing them would fix a liveness bug by adding exactly the cost this story
removes. This needs its own story.

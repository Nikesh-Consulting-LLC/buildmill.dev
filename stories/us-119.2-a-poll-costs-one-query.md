# US-119.2 — A poll costs one query

**Status:** Testing
**Phase:** 119 — The API answers while the runners poll
**Depends on:** us-119.1 (the hot paths are off the loop, so what remains to
cut is work), US-3.2 (`/worker/pool`), US-59.9 (resumable runs on the same
response), the release-prep pool listing and its reaper (`db.py:5616`),
US-7.3 (`reconcile_pushed_expired_claims` and the branch it submits)

## Story

As the manager, I want a runner asking "is there work?" to cost the factory
one query, so that eighty thousand polls a day do not each carry a
reconciler that almost never had anything to do.

## Design

### What a poll costs today

Every runner asks `GET /worker/pool` and `GET /worker/release-prep` every
`RUNNER_POLL_SECONDS` (default 3, `apps/runner/runner.py:65`) — 20–72k
requests a day of each on prod. Per `/worker/pool` (`routers/worker.py:259`):

1. `reconcile_pushed_expired_claims` runs first — a query over `runs` for
   expired-with-pushes claims, which on the overwhelming majority of polls
   finds nothing. It exists so a human who pushed and closed the laptop still
   lands in review; it does not need to run 40,000 times a day to do that.
   On the rare poll where it *does* find something it goes on to
   `perform_submit` — GitHub calls, on the request path of a runner that
   only asked for a listing.
2. `list_worker_pool`, then `list_worker_resumable`.

`/worker/release-prep` likewise runs a reaper before its listing
(`db.py:5616`, "a runner asking for work also clears the …").

Each of those is a leased connection, ~150 ms including its round trips.
The reconciler is the one that does no work for the caller.

### The change

**The reconciler and the release-prep reaper leave the request path.** One
lifespan background task, every 30 s, single-flight per process, runs
`reconcile_pushed_expired_claims` and the release-prep reaper (each through
`asyncio.to_thread`, per us-119.1's rule); a tick is time-boxed (60 s), a
failing or timed-out tick logs and the next one runs. `/worker/pool` and
`/worker/release-prep` only list. The outcome the runner cares about is
unchanged — an expired claim with pushes still auto-submits, within half a
minute rather than within one poll — and the code comments that promised
"self-healing without a background scheduler" are rewritten to say there is
one now, and where it lives.

`RUNNER_POLL_SECONDS` is deliberately **not** changed here: it is an
operator lever on the runner machines, and 3 s is the pickup latency the
manager chose.

### What this costs

- **A scheduler exists now.** The comments in `worker.py` and `db.py` said
  the listing was self-healing precisely so nobody had to run one; that
  stance is reversed and the comments must say so, or the next reader will
  add the reconciler back to the poll.
- **Auto-submit lands later.** Within ~30 s of the claim expiring instead of
  at the next 3 s poll. Nothing downstream is timed to that.
- **Per process, best effort.** An API restart skips a tick; when the API is
  down there is no sweep — but there are no polls either, so nothing is
  lost relative to today.
- **A tick that hangs must not stop the next.** Single-flight without a
  time-box would starve forever on one stuck GitHub call; the 60 s box is
  the guard, pinned by a test.

### Deferred from this story, on purpose

Two more per-request costs were measured and are real, and are **not** done
here because each opens an edge case the reconciler move does not:

- **The checkout ping on every lease** (`check=ConnectionPool.check_connection`,
  one round trip per checkout). Making it idle-aware saves ~50 ms per lease
  but hands a connection dropped in the last 30 s to a caller, who fails
  mid-request instead of at checkout — and us-117.1 is on record that this
  link hiccups.
- **A fresh `httpx.AsyncClient` per PostgREST call** (`supabase.py:64`, a
  TCP + TLS handshake each time; `/auth/me` at 170 ms bounds it). Sharing one
  saves ~50 ms per call but introduces stale keep-alives — the
  `RemoteProtocolError`/`ReadError` classes us-117.1 just learned to name —
  and US-79.5's rule is that writes never retry.

Both are named in Out of scope as a follow-up once 119.1 and this story are
measured; if the measured floor is still too high, they are the next cut.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC1 — the pool listing reconciles nothing.** `GET /worker/pool` calls
  `db.list_worker_pool` and `db.list_worker_resumable` and does not invoke
  `reconcile_pushed_expired_claims`; `GET /worker/release-prep` does not
  invoke the reaper. Pinned by route tests that assert the reconciler /
  reaper is not called during the request.
- **AC2 — the sweep runs on its own.** A lifespan task runs both every 30 s,
  single-flight, each tick time-boxed at 60 s; an exception or a timeout in
  one tick is logged and does not stop the next. Pinned by a test that
  drives three ticks — the first raising, the second exceeding the box — and
  sees the third run.
- **AC3 — the sweep obeys us-119.1.** Its database calls go through
  `asyncio.to_thread`; the AST guard's covered list includes the module the
  sweep lives in.
- **AC4 — the comments tell the truth.** The "self-healing without a
  scheduler" remarks in `worker.py` and `db.py` are replaced with a pointer
  to the sweep.
- **AC5 — measured, on prod, the day after release** (query on
  `api_request_log`, first full UTC day): DB p50 of `/worker/pool` under
  **900 ms** (from ~1.3 s — one lease fewer of three).
- **AC6 — Essential.** All new tests in the ~30 s suite; no network, no
  `DATABASE_URL`.

## Out of scope

- **The idle-aware checkout ping and the shared `httpx.AsyncClient`** —
  deferred as above; one follow-up story for both, after this phase is
  measured.
- **Push instead of poll.** The runner already holds a persistent socket
  (US-27.x); a "work available" nudge over it would let the poll interval
  grow to a minute. A story of its own — it changes the runner too.
- **`RUNNER_POLL_SECONDS`.** Operator-side; unchanged.
- **Removing `DISCARD ALL`** or re-enabling prepared statements — both
  ruled out in `pool.py`'s docstring for reasons that still hold.
- **The remaining direct `db.*` calls** outside the files us-119.1 covers.

## Verification record

Built 2026-08-18 (commit `ec7eb3f`), on us-119.1.

Two things the design above did not know and the build found:

- **There were three lazy sweeps on the poll path, not two.**
  `list_worker_pool` itself ran `requeue_expired_claims` (which runs the
  stale-heartbeat sweep inside it) before every listing, on top of the
  handler's reconciler and the release-prep listing's reaper. All three
  moved.
- **The scheduler already existed.** US-13.6's 60 s liveness loop in
  `main.py` was running the same three sweeps as a timer, and `main.py`
  ran two of them again at startup by hand. So the lazy copies were the
  third copy, not the only one. The build folds the startup and timer
  copies into the new `sweeps.run_lease_sweep` task (30 s, first tick at
  once) and leaves the liveness loop to the presence, fleet-alarm,
  agent-server and idle-session sweeps that are its own.

**Verified:**

- **AC1** — `tests/test_lease_sweep.py`: `GET /worker/pool` calls
  `list_worker_pool` and `list_worker_resumable` and trips neither the
  reconciler nor `requeue_expired_claims`; `GET /worker/release-prep` trips
  no reaper. A third test pins that the pool listing is **one** query and
  carries the paused predicate (`worker_is_paused`, the second connection,
  is deleted — nothing else called it).
- **AC2** — a fake tick that raises on the first call and hangs on the
  second: three ticks start, the third runs; a first tick starts within
  the same instant, not after an interval.
- **AC3** — `sweeps.py` is on the guard's `COVERED` list; its two
  synchronous sweeps go through `asyncio.to_thread`; a test pins that one
  tick runs exactly `requeue`, `reconcile`, `reap` in that order and reports
  its counts.
- **AC4** — a test asserts the "self-healing without a background
  scheduler" remarks are gone from `worker.py` and `db.py`, and that
  `list_worker_pool`'s and `requeue_expired_claims`'s docstrings name
  `sweeps.py`. `test_release_prep_reaper_sql.py`'s docstring updated too.
- **AC6** — Essential: **2064 passed, 591 skipped** at this commit.

**Not verified here (UAT):**

- **AC5** — DB p50 of `/worker/pool` under 900 ms on prod (same query as
  us-119.1's, first full day after release). It was ~1.3 s over five to six
  leased connections; it is now two.
- The 30 s cadence on a live box — that a requeued claim reappears in a
  runner's pool within half a minute of expiring.

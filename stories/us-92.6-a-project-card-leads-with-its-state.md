# US-92.6 — A project card leads with its state

**Status:** Testing
**Phase:** 92 — The app on a phone
**Depends on:** [us-91.9](us-91.9-a-project-card-says-which-build-is-live.md) (the live-build line this promotes)

## Story

As the manager, Projects is the page that already survives the phone: at
375px nothing overflows, the cards reflow to one column, and the deployment
tiles stack. It is the good example. It can still be better, because the
order of a card is a desktop order.

Today a card reads: name and spend, description, repo and branch, Archive and
Delete, then **People & Agents** — six chips wrapping three rows — and only
then **Deployments**, which is where the answer lives. On a phone that means
scrolling past the roster to reach "what is live", and two destructive
buttons sit above everything at full prominence.

The chips are the least urgent thing on the card and take the most room. The
live build (us-91.9) is the most urgent and comes last.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC1 — deployments come before people below `md`.** What is live on UAT
  and production is the first thing under the project's identity. The roster
  moves beneath it.
- **AC2 — People & Agents collapses to a count that expands.** `6 people &
  agents` on one line, tapping reveals the chips. Three rows of chips is a
  screenful spent on something the manager rarely acts on from a phone.
- **AC3 — Archive and Delete stop shouting.** Below `md` they move into an
  overflow (`⋯`) menu on the card header. They are rare and destructive;
  neither belongs at full size above the content on a small screen. Their
  confirmations are unchanged.
- **AC4 — spend stays visible and legible.** The `$34.00` figure keeps its
  place beside the name — it is a headline number, and its budget-state
  colouring must survive.
- **AC5 — the deployment tile keeps everything us-91.9 gave it**: `Live:
  <version> · deployed <when>` with the version linking to its release, the
  environment badge, the URL, and the last-run dot. Only the arrangement may
  change — a tile at 311px can afford two lines.
- **AC6 — the card is one tap from the project.** Its name is a link at a
  real tap size; today the card's interactive parts are the repo link, the
  two buttons and the deployment tiles, and the project itself is easy to
  miss.
- **AC7 — `md`+ is unchanged.** Same order, same chips, same buttons.

## Out of scope

- The project detail page and its tabs.
- Changing what a project card shows on desktop, or adding new facts to it.
- The Archived tab's layout.

## Verified (2026-08-14, localhost at 375x812)

- **Deployments now come first**: the Deployments heading sits at y=487 and the
  roster's summary at y=696 on the same card. One `order` on each block rather
  than a second copy of either.
- **The roster is collapsed** to `1 people & agents` (or "No people or agents"
  when empty) and opens on tap.
- **Archive and Delete measure 0px until opened** behind the `⋯` button. They
  were behind a closed `<details>` first, which still laid its children out —
  two destructive buttons were on screen at 34px wide while claiming to be
  hidden. Explicit state replaced it; the two buttons are declared once and
  placed twice, so phone and desktop cannot offer different actions.
